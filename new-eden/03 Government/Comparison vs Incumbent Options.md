# Comparison vs Incumbent Options

← [[Government Home]]

> **Framing note:** No named competitor products or sourced third-party benchmarking data were used to build this comparison. The rows below describe **generic incumbent categories** — canvas tents, converted shipping containers without structural engineering, caravans, and basic demountables — as archetypes commonly used in disaster response today, for the purpose of illustrating where the NE-DR-401's design choices matter operationally. Figures for the NE-DR-401 are the stated engineering facts from [[Government Home]]; figures and characterisations for incumbent categories are qualitative and illustrative, not measured. Any procurement evaluation should substitute real quotes, test data, and agency-specific benchmarks before this table informs a decision.

## Comparison dimensions

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## How to read this table

This is a **category comparison**, not a procurement scorecard. Its purpose is to make explicit the trade-offs an agency is implicitly making today when it fields tents, ad hoc container conversions, caravans, or demountables in a disaster-response role — and to show which of those trade-offs the NE-DR-401's design choices are aimed at closing:

- **Setup time and transportability** are addressed by the ISO chassis and plug-and-play utility connections — see [[Deployment Value Proposition]].
- **Standards compliance and weatherproofing** are addressed by building to the NCC/AS-NZS framework as a floor rather than a target — see [[Compliance for Emergency Life-Safety Use]].
- **Dignity of accommodation** is a direct consequence of sharing a fit-out lineage with a premium residential product rather than being a stripped-down emergency-only build.
- **Reusability and lifespan** feed the acquisition and budget conversation in [[Total Cost of Ownership Model]] and the fleet model in [[Procurement & Fleet Model]].

## What this comparison cannot tell you

It cannot tell you unit cost, delivered lead time, or how the NE-DR-401 performs against a specific named product an agency currently uses — those require real quotes and, ideally, side-by-side trials. It also does not model mixed-fleet scenarios (e.g. a tent surge capacity behind a smaller core of NE-DR-401 units), which is a realistic procurement pattern worth exploring separately in [[Procurement & Fleet Model]].
