# Premium-Beyond-Minimum Design Philosophy

Founder's design mandate: Australian standards (the NCC and AS/NZS suite documented in [[Standards & Compliance Baseline]]) are the **floor**, not the target. This is non-negotiable for two independent reasons that apply to New Eden's two product lines respectively — the residential line is a premium brand that cannot compete on "meets code" against custom architecture, and the Disaster Relief Pod is life-safety equipment where "meets code" is an unacceptably low bar for something emergency services will stake operational continuity on. This note translates that mandate into concrete, checkable design decisions across structure, materials, weatherproofing, and lifespan — every claim here should be treated as the standard other vaults (investor, self-validation, government) measure specific product claims against.

## Structure

- **Wind and structural load design targeted to the highest classification the product is sold into, not the site-average classification.** Per [[Standards & Compliance Baseline]], AS/NZS 1170.2 wind loading is site-specific; New Eden's stated philosophy is to engineer the shared chassis and framing system (see [[Dimensions & ISO Compatibility]]) to a cyclonic-region wind rating as the baseline manufacturing spec, rather than manufacturing a cheaper "standard wind region" version and a separate cyclonic-rated version. This is what allows a single factory-standard SKU (see [[Manufacturing Process]]) to be sold and deployed anywhere in Australia — including the Disaster Relief Pod's need to be dispatched to whichever region has just had a cyclone — without a slower, bespoke re-engineering step per order.
- **Stacking rating held at a genuine safety margin above the marketed "3 high."** Marketing claims "engineered stackable up to 3 high"; the design philosophy this note documents is that the ISO 1161 corner-casting load path and chassis frame (see [[Dimensions & ISO Compatibility]]) should be certified with a margin above the 3-high working load (analogous to how lifting equipment is rated to a working load limit well below its actual failure load), so that 3-high stacking is a comfortable, repeatable operating condition rather than the frame's absolute limit.
- **Cold-formed steel framing (AS/NZS 4600, see [[Standards & Compliance Baseline]]) specified for fatigue, not just static load.** Because every module is subjected to repeated transport-induced vibration and handling loads over a 25+ year service life (explicitly stated for the Disaster Relief Pod, and a reasonable equivalent target for the residential line), the framing spec needs to account for cyclic fatigue from repeated relocation, not just a one-time static design load — a materially harder target than a site-built home's structure ever has to meet, since a site-built frame is assembled once and never trucked or craned again.

## Materials

- **All-non-combustible external material palette as a bushfire-performance decision, not just an aesthetic one.** The Zinc Monument / Woodland Grey / Copper Natural / Night Sky palette (see [[Materials & Finishes]]) being entirely steel and copper rather than including any combustible cladding option is a deliberate exceed-the-floor decision against AS 3959 (see [[Standards & Compliance Baseline]]) — it means New Eden can credibly market into the highest Bushfire Attack Level categories without a lesser-performing material option ever being on the table to begin with.
- **Marine-duty corrosion protection as the standard finish system, not an optional upgrade.** Feature 8 (black steel base, see [[Product Line - Residential]]) and the platform-wide chassis coating are specified in this vault as marine-duty corrosion systems specifically because every unit is expected to be transported by sea at some point in its life (per [[Dimensions & ISO Compatibility]]'s "sea freight compatible" claim) and, for the Disaster Relief Pod, may sit in coastal, floodwater, or storage-yard exposure for years between deployments (see [[Product Line - Disaster Relief Pod]]) — a standard architectural coating system sized for a fixed suburban site would be under-specified for either product line's real exposure profile.
- **Factory-patinated copper finishes (see [[Materials & Finishes]]) treated as a durability decision as well as an aesthetic one.** Selecting a stable, factory-applied patination (Weathered/Verdigris) rather than leaving copper to age naturally and unevenly on site removes decades of unpredictable weathering variance from the building's appearance — a premium-brand consistency guarantee that a builder using raw copper cannot offer.

## Weatherproofing

- **Rainwater and overflow capacity sized above AS/NZS 3500.3 minimums.** As noted in [[Product Line - Residential]] feature 5 (secret rainheads) and [[Standards & Compliance Baseline]], the hidden-gutter and rainhead system is specified with generous overflow capacity precisely because it is concealed — a visible, exposed gutter overflowing is an obvious maintenance signal to an occupant, but a concealed parapet gutter overflowing has real potential to cause hidden water ingress before anyone notices, so the concealment design choice itself creates an obligation to over-spec the hydraulic capacity rather than just meet the calculated minimum.
- **Watertightness verified by a physical test, not just a design calculation.** [[Manufacturing Process]] Stage 6's QC gate is documented in this vault as an actual flood/spray test on the completed roof and gutter assembly before interior fit-out, rather than relying solely on a compliant design and installation checklist — every unit is empirically proven watertight at the factory, which a site-built home essentially never is (site-built roofs are rarely spray-tested; defects are typically found by the first heavy rain after occupancy).
- **Rainscreen drainage cavity behind every shadow-gap cladding joint, not a face-sealed system.** Feature 3 ([[Product Line - Residential]]) specifies a pressure-equalized drainage cavity behind the cladding reveal rather than relying purely on sealant to keep water out at the joint — a two-line-of-defence approach (drainage plus a well-detailed joint) that exceeds what many face-sealed cladding systems on the market rely on as their sole weatherproofing strategy.

## Lifespan

- **25+ years is stated for the Disaster Relief Pod under a harder degradation profile than continuous residential occupancy** (intermittent storage, repeated transport-and-redeployment cycles, potential floodwater/saltwater exposure — see [[Product Line - Disaster Relief Pod]]). The residential line, which experiences continuous maintained occupancy and (for most units) a single fixed siting after initial placement, should be held to at least the same 25+ year design-life target, and the materials/coating choices above (marine-duty corrosion protection, non-combustible cladding, empirically tested watertightness) are what make that target credible under either usage pattern rather than only under the easier one.
- **Digital twin lifecycle tracking (see [[Product Line - Residential]] feature 16 and [[Factory & Automation Stack]]) is itself a lifespan-extending design decision, not just a traceability feature.** Because every module's as-built and as-tested record is retained and linked to a physical serial number, in-service maintenance (via the robotic service dock and magnetic service wall, features 14–15) can be targeted and evidence-based — a service technician knows the exact original specification and prior service history rather than having to inspect and guess, which materially improves the odds that a 25-year design life is actually achieved rather than merely claimed, and gives New Eden's service network the same evidence base a technician would need on an active life-safety asset.

## The one governing rule for other vaults
Wherever the investor, self-validation, or government vaults need to cite a New Eden performance number (wind rating, stacking capacity, watertightness, service life, corrosion protection), the number should be presented as exceeding the relevant AS/NZS floor documented in [[Standards & Compliance Baseline]], with the specific margin and mechanism traceable back to this note — "meets code" should never appear as a standalone claim anywhere in the New Eden documentation set.
