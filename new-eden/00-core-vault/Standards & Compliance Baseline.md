# Standards & Compliance Baseline

This note lays out the regulatory **floor** New Eden operates above — the National Construction Code (NCC) classification considerations and the applicable AS/NZS standard families that govern different parts of the build. Per the founder's design mandate (see [[Premium-Beyond-Minimum Design Philosophy]]), meeting these standards is necessary but explicitly **not** the target anywhere in the New Eden product range — it is the baseline every other note in this vault should be read against. This note deliberately stays at the level of "which standard family governs which part of the build and why," rather than citing specific clause numbers that would need verification against a current code edition.

## NCC classification considerations for a relocatable/modular chassis product

- **Building classification** is the first question any modular/relocatable building manufacturer has to resolve, and it is not a single fixed answer for New Eden because the same chassis serves two very different use cases. The residential line is most consistent with **Class 1a** (a single dwelling) treatment when installed as a standalone or expanded-module home, though a multi-unit or short-term-letting deployment could push a configuration toward Class 1b or Class 3 territory — classification is use-driven, not just building-form-driven, so it needs to be confirmed per deployment scenario rather than asserted once for the whole product line.
- **Relocatable/transportable building provisions.** Because every New Eden unit is manufactured off-site and transported as a complete or near-complete module (see [[Dimensions & ISO Compatibility]]), it falls within the NCC's and the states'/territories' treatment of transportable/relocatable buildings, which generally requires: compliance certification tied to the manufacturing facility (consistent with New Eden's factory inspection/sign-off process — see [[Manufacturing Process]] Stage 8 and the factory signature wall in [[Product Line - Residential]] feature 25), plus a separate site-specific certification step once the unit is installed (footings/tie-downs, service connections, and any site-specific wind/soil classification) — the factory build quality does not, by itself, complete the compliance chain; the site installation step is a distinct regulatory event.
- **The Disaster Relief Pod sits partly outside standard NCC occupancy assumptions.** Emergency-services deployable structures used for short-duration, non-permanent occupancy are frequently treated differently under state emergency-management and works-approval frameworks than a permanent Class 1a dwelling — this is a genuine open question for the government/disaster-relief vault to resolve with the relevant state/territory authority per deployment, not something this vault should assert a single fixed answer for. What is fixed here: the structural, electrical, and life-safety standards below still apply in substance even where the occupancy-approval pathway differs.

## AS/NZS standard families governing the build

**Structural actions & wind loading — AS/NZS 1170 series.** Governs the general structural design actions (permanent, imposed, wind) that every load-bearing element of the chassis, wall framing, and roof trusses must be designed against. The wind-loading component of this family is the one most directly relevant to New Eden's Australia-wide (and cyclonic-region) deployment ambitions — a unit intended to be sited anywhere from a temperate suburb to a cyclone-classified coastal region needs its structural design (and, specifically, feature-level details like the glass garage door and continuous window band in [[Product Line - Residential]]) verified against the highest wind classification it will realistically be sold or deployed into, not just a baseline region.

**Bushfire-prone-area construction — AS 3959.** Directly relevant given Australian deployment: this standard governs construction requirements for buildings in designated bushfire-prone areas (ember attack, radiant heat, and in higher categories direct flame contact resistance for elements like windows, doors, and cladding junctions). New Eden's material palette (see [[Materials & Finishes]]) is entirely non-combustible steel and copper, which is a materially strong starting position against this standard family compared to timber-clad competitors — but bushfire compliance is also detail-driven (gap sizing at cladding joints, ember-proofing of vents and the recessed entry/shutter cavities, etc.), so the shadow-gap and hidden-roller-shutter details in [[Product Line - Residential]] need to be checked at the highest applicable Bushfire Attack Level (BAL) the product is marketed into, not just the base material choice.

**Cold-formed steel structures — AS/NZS 4600.** Directly relevant given the roll-formed framing approach described throughout [[Manufacturing Process]] and [[Factory & Automation Stack]] — this standard family governs the structural design of cold-formed (roll-formed/light-gauge) steel members, which is exactly what the FrameCAD-class roll-forming process produces for New Eden's floor joists, wall studs, and roof truss chords. Because the entire structural system is cold-formed steel rather than timber or hot-rolled steel, this standard family (rather than the timber-framing code) is the primary structural code the framing stages of manufacturing are designed to.

**Accessibility — AS 1428 series.** Governs accessible design provisions (thresholds, door widths, circulation, sanitary facilities). Most directly relevant to: (a) any residential configuration marketed or required to be accessible, where the recessed entry (feature 2) threshold detail needs to resolve a level, wheelchair-accessible transition rather than a raised sill; and (b) the Disaster Relief Pod's Medical and Accommodation configurations, where emergency-services deployment standards typically expect accessible egress and, for Medical, accessible treatment-bay clearances as a matter of operational necessity as much as regulatory requirement.

**Electrical wiring rules — AS/NZS 3000 ("Wiring Rules").** Governs the electrical installation work embedded across [[Manufacturing Process]] Stage 7 — switchboard, circuit design, and the smart ceiling's integrated lighting/sensor wiring (see [[Product Line - Residential]] feature 26) all need to be designed and, critically, *inspected and certified* against this standard, which is one of the clearer arguments in [[Factory & Automation Stack]] for why full "zero human input" is not realistic at final QA — electrical work in Australia generally requires licensed-electrician sign-off regardless of how the physical installation was performed.

**Gas installation rules — AS/NZS 5601.** Relevant wherever a configuration includes gas appliances (hot water, cooking, or — plausibly — a Medical-configuration medical gas or backup gas-fired generation system on the Disaster Relief Pod). Given New Eden's platform emphasizes hidden/integrated services (hidden air conditioning, magnetic service wall) and an all-electric-friendly design language (solar roof skin, battery-friendly hidden AC), it is a reasonable product decision to minimize or eliminate gas dependency where possible — reducing this standard family's footprint on the product is itself a viable "exceeds minimum" simplification the [[Premium-Beyond-Minimum Design Philosophy]] note should consider recommending.

## Standards not explicitly requested but implied by features already documented
- **AS/NZS 3500 series (plumbing and drainage)** — implicated directly by the hidden gutters, secret rainheads, and stormwater routing detailed in [[Product Line - Residential]] (features 4, 5), and by the water/waste service stub-outs on both product lines (see [[Dimensions & ISO Compatibility]]).
- **AS 4055 / cyclonic wind classification** — the practical mechanism by which a specific site's AS/NZS 1170.2 wind loading is translated into a residential-scale wind classification (N1–N6/C1–C4), directly relevant to which glazing, roofing, and fixing specifications a given New Eden unit needs at a given deployment address.
- **Glass and glazing — AS 1288 / AS 2047** — relevant to every corner-glass, window-band, and glass-garage-door feature in [[Product Line - Residential]], governing glass selection, edge cover, and framing for the applicable wind/human-impact safety class.

## How to use this note
Downstream vaults (especially the government/disaster-relief vault) should cite this note as "the floor," and should not present NCC/AS-NZS compliance itself as a competitive claim — it is the entry ticket. The actual competitive/trust claim is the margin above this floor, documented in [[Premium-Beyond-Minimum Design Philosophy]].
