# Compliance for Emergency Life-Safety Use

← [[Home]]

The NE-DR-401 is not a general-purpose modular building deployed in ordinary conditions — it is a life-safety product deployed specifically **into** the acute conditions that trigger an emergency response. That reframes what "standards compliance" needs to mean for this unit, which is the founder position stated on [[Home]]: Australian NCC and AS/NZS standards are treated as the **regulatory floor, not the target**.

> **Standards note:** This document describes the standards *families* relevant to the NE-DR-401 and why each matters for emergency use, at the level of "this standard governs X, here is why it matters here." It does not cite specific clause numbers, editions, or test results, and should not be read as a certification claim. Formal compliance and certification documentation is a separate deliverable from this vault.

## Why the bar is higher here than on the residential line

A residential module is expected to be commissioned once, in planned conditions, and to sit largely undisturbed for years. The NE-DR-401 is expected to be:

- Repeatedly connected and disconnected from site services in field conditions, often by personnel other than a qualified electrician or plumber attending a fixed job.
- Deployed in direct response to the same wind, storm, and flood events that structural and wind-loading standards are designed against — meaning the worst-case load scenario for the building code is also the most likely deployment trigger for the unit.
- Occupied, in the Medical and Accommodation configurations, by people who did not choose to be there and may include the injured, elderly, very young, or people with mobility constraints — a population the building code's minimum accessibility provisions may under-serve if treated as a ceiling rather than a floor.
- Used clinically, in the Medical configuration, where infection control is not an amenity consideration but a patient-safety one.

## Standards families relevant to the NE-DR-401

### Accessibility — AS 1428 family
Governs accessible design: clear width of doorways and circulation space, ramp gradients, accessible sanitary facilities, and related provisions. For a unit that may serve as a Medical or Accommodation space in the first hours after a disaster, accessibility cannot be assumed away because occupants are "temporary" — the population needing triage, treatment, or emergency housing after a bushfire or flood event includes people with existing mobility constraints, people newly injured, and elderly evacuees who are statistically over-represented in disaster-affected populations. Egress steps and entry provisions are part of the unit's standard fit-out (see [[Home]]); the accessibility standard is the reference point for whether that provision is sufficient for the intended use, not just for a minimum commercial fit-out.

### Structural wind and general actions — AS/NZS 1170 family
Governs structural design actions, including wind loading. This is the standard most directly connected to the unit's actual use case: the wind-loading provisions exist to keep a structure standing in a storm, and the NE-DR-401 is specifically intended to be staged and occupied around cyclone, severe storm, and flood events — the same event classes that drive wind design categories in the first place. A structure whose worst-case load scenario coincides with its primary deployment trigger needs its structural rating treated as a first-order design input, not a compliance checkbox applied after the fact.

### Electrical safety — AS/NZS 3000 (wiring rules) family
Governs safe electrical installation and connection practice. The NE-DR-401's plug-and-play exterior power service ports are designed for repeated field connection and disconnection — potentially in wet conditions, by generator supply rather than grid power, and by personnel under operational time pressure. The wiring-rules framework is the reference for ensuring that repeated field connection remains safe connection, including protection against fault conditions that matter more, not less, when the surrounding environment is a disaster response site.

### Gas safety — AS/NZS 5601 (gas installations) family
Governs the safe installation and use of gas fittings and appliances where the unit configuration involves gas (e.g. cooking or heating provisions in Accommodation or Support roles). The same field-conditions logic applies as for electrical safety: a fitting designed and tested for a static residential install needs the same safety margin, or better, when it is part of a unit that may be relocated and reconnected multiple times across its 25+ year service life.

### Hygiene and infection control — Medical configuration
Not a single named AS/NZS standard but a set of hygiene-relevant design considerations that matter specifically for the Medical role configuration: surfaces and finishes that can be cleaned and disinfected between uses, separation of clean and contaminated workflows within the available floor plan, and provisions that support consistent infection-control practice in a field clinical setting rather than a hospital. This is a design and specification question for the Medical fit-out as much as a standards-citation question, and should be validated with the relevant health authority's field-clinic infection-control guidance during procurement.

## What this means for procurement evaluation

An agency evaluating the NE-DR-401 should treat NCC/AS-NZS compliance as the minimum entry bar to even be considered for a life-safety emergency role, and ask New Eden to demonstrate, unit by unit and configuration by configuration, where the build exceeds that floor — particularly on accessibility provision in Medical/Accommodation configurations, wind rating margin relative to the event classes the unit is meant to be deployed into, and field-connection safety for the plug-and-play electrical and (where fitted) gas services. The factory QR digital-twin tracking described in [[Procurement & Fleet Model]] is the mechanism by which inspection and compliance status can be verified per-unit across a distributed fleet over the unit's service life, not just at time of delivery.
